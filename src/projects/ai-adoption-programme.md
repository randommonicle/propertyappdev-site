## Starting without a brief

The work of a property manager has a lot of repeatable, high-stakes writing in it: Section 20 consultation notices, AGM packs, replies to conveyancers' LPE1 enquiries, letters to boards about their obligations. It follows patterns, and it has to be right. I thought AI could take a real share of that drafting if it was introduced properly, so I wrote an AI adoption strategy and presented it to the senior partners.

## The pilot

The rollout began as a five-seat pilot of Claude. I ran the practical side myself, including seat allocation, billing and access controls, and wrote prompt frameworks and workflow guides for the work we actually do: LPE1 enquiries, Section 20 documentation, correspondence and lease interpretation. The pilot then became a firm-wide rollout.

## Data protection

A firm like ours holds personal data about leaseholders, directors and contractors, so the programme came with its own data protection documentation: a record of processing activities, a legitimate interests assessment and a data protection impact assessment. Between them they cover what personal data may go into the model and on what lawful basis.

## Training and checking

I built a staff training pack, facilitator scripts for the sessions and an onboarding tracker. Alongside them sits a verification framework that governs how AI-assisted work is checked. In a regulated firm a draft is only useful if everyone knows who checked it and against what.

## What came out of it

Four production tools built in the programme are in daily use across the team, supporting statutory documentation, governance correspondence, house style and compliance work. Running costs are attributed per workflow and reviewed against the time the tools save. The [property inspection app](/projects/property-inspection-app/) belongs to the same programme. Work I started on my own initiative is now part of my formal job title.
